About

About Canine Science Forum

The Canine Science Forum (CSF) is the premier international interdisciplinary conference dedicated to the biology, ecology, and behaviour of dogs, wolves, and other canids. Established in 2008 and held biennially, it serves as the definitive platform for scientists to share cutting-edge research and foster global collaboration.

Our Mission

Mission & Objectives

The Forum promotes canine biology as a vital model for scientific investigation while leveraging evidence-based knowledge to improve animal welfare and human-canine integration.

Advancing Research

Consolidating canine biology as a rigorous, interdisciplinary field.

Societal Integration

Enhancing the role of dogs in a rapidly evolving modern world.

Ethical Innovation

Promoting non-invasive research technologies and best scientific practices.

Welfare & Education

Spreading awareness of the beneficial roles dogs play in society.

Scientific Scope

Tinbergen's Four Questions

Our program is structured around Tinbergen's Four Questions, ensuring a comprehensive exploration of canine science.

Mechanisms

The physiological and neurological basis of behaviour.

Ontogeny

The development and growth of canids throughout their lifespan.

Function

The adaptive value and purpose of specific traits and behaviours.

Evolution

The phylogenetic history and selective pressures shaping the species.

Audience

Who Comes to CSF?

Scientists

Academic leaders and visionaries. Share your latest empirical findings, challenge existing paradigms, and shape the theoretical future of canine science alongside the world's top experts.

Researchers

The engine of data and discovery. Present your methodologies, get expert peer feedback on your research design, and find collaborators to scale your impact across 35+ countries.

Speakers

Leading Voices in Canine Science

We are assembling a program of leading researchers and experienced practitioners. Confirmed speakers will be announced as they are finalized.

Dr. Emily Bray
University of Arizona
Dr. Emily Bray
Assistant Professor · University of Arizona, College of Veterinary Medicine · USA
Dr. Emily Bray is a distinguished expert in canine behavior and cognition, specializing in the developmental trajectories of working dogs. Her research explores how biological and environmental factors influence problem-solving abilities throughout an animal's life. By integrating psychology with veterinary science, she provides critical insights that optimize both canine training and human-animal interactions.
Dr. Anna Zamansky
University of Haifa
Dr. Anna Zamansky
Assistant Professor & Head of Tech4Animals Lab · University of Haifa · Israel
Dr. Anna Zamansky is a pioneer in animal-centered computing, leading the Tech4Animals Lab in bridging the gap between AI and animal welfare. Her groundbreaking research focuses on developing machine learning models that detect pain and emotions through the analysis of facial expressions and body language. She collaborates with international NGOs to create innovative digital solutions for monitoring the well-being of both domestic and farm animals.
Prof. Paola Maria Valsecchi
University of Parma
Prof. Paola Maria Valsecchi
Associate Professor of Applied Ethology · University of Parma · Italy
A renowned ethologist specializing in social learning, canine welfare in shelters, and the psychological attachment between humans and dogs. A prolific researcher and international collaborator, she has contributed extensively to our understanding of temperament and behavioral dynamics across various species. Her work combines rigorous biological observation with a dedicated focus on improving the quality of life for domestic animals.
Prof. Paolo Mongillo
University of Padua
Prof. Paolo Mongillo
Associate Professor of Veterinary Physiology · University of Padua · Italy
A leading specialist in the physiological and cognitive aspects of domestic animals, with a particular focus on canine visual perception and aging. As president of the Center for Canine Behavioural Studies, he translates academic research from the DogUP Lab into practical education for animal professionals. His research is key to understanding how an animal's physiological state influences its emotional well-being and interaction with the environment.
Dr. John-Tyler Binfet
University of British Columbia
Dr. John-Tyler Binfet
Associate Professor & Director of B.A.R.K. · University of British Columbia · Canada
A prominent scholar in canine-assisted interventions and founder of UBC’s B.A.R.K. program, supporting student emotional health. Professor and Director of Graduate Programs at UBC, he is the author of three books on therapy dogs and canine welfare. His research provides a rigorous scientific framework for integrating dogs into educational settings to foster well-being.
Prof. Aubrey Fine
California State Polytechnic University
Prof. Aubrey Fine
Professor Emeritus, Adjunct Professor & Psychologist · California State Polytechnic University & Utah State University · USA
Globally renowned pioneer in human-animal interactions, recipient of the William McCulloch Award. His research focuses on Animal Assisted Services for youth resilience and ADHD. Author of the seminal Handbook on Animal Assisted Therapy, influencing welfare standards worldwide.
Karolina Westlund
Ethology & Animal Welfare
Karolina Westlund
Associate Professor of Ethology · Animal Behavior Educator
Karolina Westlund is an associate professor of ethology and educator in animal behavior and welfare. Her interdisciplinary approach combines applied ethology, behavior analysis, and affective neuroscience to promote humane, evidence-based training. Through lectures and online courses, she helps pet owners and animal professionals support animals that are well behaved, happy, and able to thrive in human care.
Dr. Alexandra (Sasha) Protopopova
University of British Columbia
Dr. Alexandra (Sasha) Protopopova
Associate Professor · University of British Columbia · Canada
Dr. Alexandra (Sasha) Protopopova is an Associate Professor in the Animal Welfare Program at the University of British Columbia. Her One Welfare research connects the needs of animals, people, and the environment. A Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist, she combines science and community engagement to improve sheltering, training, and human-animal interactions.
Dr. Tamás Faragó
Eötvös Loránd University
Dr. Tamás Faragó
Senior Research Fellow & Principal Investigator, BARKS Lab · Eötvös Loránd University · Hungary
Dr. Tamás Faragó is a senior researcher at Eötvös Loránd University and Principal Investigator of the BARKS Lab. His work explores canine vocal communication and the cognitive and neural mechanisms behind human-dog communication. Combining ethology, acoustics, brain imaging, genetics, and machine learning, he leads the ERC-funded K9VocLearn project on vocal learning in dogs.
Magdalena Boch
University of Vienna
Magdalena Boch
Assistant Professor · University of Vienna & Messerli Research Institute · Austria
An Assistant Professor at the University of Vienna and Messerli Research Institute, she heads the Social Neuroecology Lab and directs the Comparative Canine Neuroimaging Unit. Her research uses awake dog neuroimaging to explore how dogs and humans perceive their social environments, advancing our understanding of the social brain across carnivoran species. Recipient of the 2024 L'Oréal-UNESCO For Women in Science Award and elected Fellow of the Linnean Society of London.
Dr. Else Verbeek
Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences
Dr. Else Verbeek
Associate Professor & Founder of Amazing Animal Minds · Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences · Sweden
An Associate Professor at the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences in Uppsala and founder of Amazing Animal Minds. Her research addresses key welfare questions — including choice, agency, time perception, and the impact of stress, hunger, and fireworks across species. Through Amazing Animal Minds, she bridges the gap between science and practice, translating cutting-edge research into practical training for animal behaviour professionals.

The Host City

Warsaw: A Sophisticated Hub
of Science and Heritage

Experience a city where profound history meets the forefront of European innovation — a resilient intellectual center that seamlessly blends scholarly traditions with a dynamic, modern academic landscape.

The Academic Vibe

Warsaw offers a refined atmosphere for global thinkers — a city of contrasts, where meticulously restored historic districts coexist with a high-tech skyline, creating an inspiring environment for professional networking and scholarly reflection.

Intellectual Serenity by the Vistula

The Vistula riverbanks provide a sophisticated space for relaxation. The west bank offers elegant promenades and modernist architecture, while the east bank remains a rare, natural riparian forest — perfect for quiet contemplation.

A Global Intersection of Culture

From the Fryderyk Chopin Museum to its acclaimed culinary scene and neo-classical parks, Warsaw offers an enriching experience for those who appreciate both scientific rigor and aesthetic depth.

Exclusive Institutional Access

Delegates will engage with Warsaw's leading scientific community, including curated visits to top-tier facilities such as the Children's Memorial Health Institute — an insider's perspective on Poland's clinical excellence.

The official language of the conference is English. All presentations, posters, and printed materials will be provided in English.

Simultaneous interpretation into Polish will be available for all sessions.

CSF 2027 is an in-person event. We believe the best connections happen face to face — especially the hallway conversations that make conferences truly valuable.

Unfortunately, dogs are not permitted inside the University of Warsaw building. If you are traveling with your dog, please arrange care in advance.

Poland is a member of the Schengen Area.

Visa-free countries (USA, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, and 60+ others): No visa required for stays up to 90 days. Note: ETIAS may become mandatory for visa-exempt travelers in 2027.

Visa-required countries: Apply for a short-stay Schengen visa (Type C) at your nearest Polish embassy at least 6 weeks before travel. Fee: €80.
