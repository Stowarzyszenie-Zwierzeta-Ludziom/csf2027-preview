Call for Abstracts

Submit your abstract

Present your research at the 10th Canine Science Forum as an oral presentation, a Pecha Kucha presentation or a poster. Abstracts are submitted and reviewed on OpenReview.

  • Deadline 28 February 2027, 23:00 CET (UTC+1)
  • Max. 5,000 characters
  • Warsaw, 14–17 June 2027

How to submit

Four steps

Please read all four before you start.

  1. Step 01Do this first

    Get your OpenReview profile ready

    You need an active profile to submit. Details below.

  2. Step 02

    Prepare

    Title, co-authors’ profiles, keywords and the abstract (max. 5,000 characters).

  3. Step 03

    Submit on OpenReview

    Fill in the form on the CSF 2027 page on OpenReview and submit. The confirmation e-mail with your submission number confirms receipt, not acceptance.

    Go to OpenReview
  4. Step 04

    Edit until the deadline

    Edit or remove your submission in your Author Console until 28 Feb 2027, 23:00 CET. After that, it goes to review.

Step 1 in detail: your OpenReview profile

Recommended: create it by 10 January 2027.

  1. 1. Sign up at openreview.net/signup, with your institutional e-mail if you can
  2. 2. Confirm your e-mail with the link in the “OpenReview Signup Confirmation” message
  3. 3. Complete your profile current position, institution and a homepage, then register
  4. 4. Activation (a separate step)
    E-mail from an institution OpenReview knows: usually quick Gmail and similar, or an institution not yet recognised: manual approval, a few days
  • Homepage

    A page that shows your name, affiliation and the e-mail you registered with, e.g. your institutional staff page.

  • No institution?

    Sign up as an Independent Researcher.

  • Already on OpenReview?

    Use your existing profile and update your institution and e-mail. Do not create a second profile.

  • Co-authors

    Every author needs an OpenReview profile before you submit, so ask your co-authors to create theirs early. In the “Authors” field, search for them by name or profile ID (e.g. ~Jane_Smith1), not by e-mail.

Show the steps as text
  1. Open Sign Up. On openreview.net, click “Login”, then “Sign Up” under “New User?”. Each author needs their own profile. One profile works for all conferences on OpenReview.
  2. Full name. Type your name exactly as it appears on your papers, then tick the box. Changing your name later needs approval by OpenReview staff.
  3. Date of birth. Choose the month, then type the day and the year. OpenReview uses it only to check your age. It is never shown publicly.
  4. E-mail. Enter the e-mail address for your profile and click “Sign Up”. Use your institutional e-mail if you can. With Gmail and similar addresses this warning appears and activation is manual, which can take a few days.
  5. Password. Choose a password, repeat it and click “Sign Up”. 10–64 characters, with at least one uppercase letter, one lowercase letter and one digit.
  6. Confirm your name. Tick “I confirm my name is correct”, complete the security check and click “Register”. This creates your account.
  7. Check your e-mail. Open the e-mail “OpenReview signup confirmation” and click the link in it. No e-mail? Check your spam folder. Delivery can take up to 8 hours.
  8. Names. The link opens “Complete Registration”, in six sections. Check your name. Add other names you have published under, if any.
  9. Personal Info. Click “Next Section”. Gender and pronouns are optional; check your date of birth. Only the date of birth is required here.
  10. Emails. Your sign-up e-mail is already confirmed. Add other addresses you use or have used. Addresses from former jobs help OpenReview recognise you and avoid duplicate profiles.
  11. Personal Links. Enter at least one link to a page that shows your name and e-mail, for example your staff page. Required. It helps OpenReview confirm who you are.
  12. Career & Education History. Add your current position: position, start year, institution domain, name and country. Leave “End” empty. Required. The institution domain is used to detect conflicts of interest.
  13. Expertise. Optional: add your research areas, then click “Register for OpenReview”. By registering, you accept OpenReview’s Terms of Use.
  14. Wait for activation. Wait for the e-mail saying your profile is active. Then you can log in and submit. With an institutional e-mail this is usually quick; otherwise it can take a few days.

Show the steps as text
  1. Not logged in? Clicking “CSF 2027 Conference Submission” only shows “Please Login to proceed”. The form opens only for logged-in users with an active OpenReview profile.
  2. Log in. When your profile is active, click “Login” and sign in with your e-mail and password. Then go back to the CSF 2027 page: the top bar shows your name.
  3. Open the form. Click “CSF 2027 Conference Submission” again. The form opens below the button.
  4. Title. Type the title only. Review is anonymous, so no author names. Max. 250 characters.
  5. Authors. You are already listed. Find each co-author by name or profile ID and click +. All authors must have an OpenReview profile.
  6. Keywords. Add a few keywords, separated by commas. The form requires them.
  7. TL;DR. Optional: one sentence about your study. You can leave it empty.
  8. Abstract. Paste the five sections with their headings. Max. 5,000 characters, headings and spaces included. The counter shows what is left.
  9. Email Sharing. Tick the box. All author e-mails are shared with the Program Chairs.
  10. Data Release. Tick the box. Accepted abstracts and author names may be released publicly after the conference.
  11. License. Choose CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. It is the only option. You keep the copyright; others may share your abstract only unchanged, with credit, and not commercially.
  12. Readers and Signatures. Readers are set by the form. Under Signatures, choose your own profile if it is not selected yet. This records who made the submission.
  13. Submit. Click Submit and wait for the confirmation. The confirmation e-mail confirms receipt, not acceptance.
  14. After submitting. Edit or remove your submission in your Author Console until 28 Feb 2027, 23:00 CET. After the deadline, it goes to review.

Abstracts are submitted under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives).

You keep the copyright. The licence does not transfer it to anyone, and you remain free to publish the full study elsewhere. If you plan a journal article, check that journal’s policy on prior conference abstracts.

What others may do: share your abstract unchanged, with credit to the authors, for non-commercial purposes. What they may not do: use it commercially, or distribute a changed version of it.

Why this licence: OpenReview requires every submission to carry a licence and offers Creative Commons licences. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 is the most restrictive of them. It comes closest to how conferences in our field usually publish abstracts: free to read, with no reuse rights for others. The default option, CC BY 4.0, would also allow commercial use and changed versions.

Visibility is a separate matter. The licence does not by itself make your abstract public; see “Who can see my abstract?” below. OpenReview’s Terms of Use also dedicate submission metadata, which they define to include the abstract, to the public domain (CC0) to the extent the submitter holds rights in it; we are clarifying with OpenReview how this applies to abstract-only submissions.

Before the deadline: you, your co-authors and the organisers. After the deadline: the organisers; all Scientific Board members may see the anonymised titles and abstracts to indicate which ones they can review; then the reviewers assigned to your abstract. Author names are hidden from reviewers.
